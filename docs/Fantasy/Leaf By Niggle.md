# Leaf by Niggle by J.R.R. Tolkien

## Overview
It's a story about an artist who never manages to finish his life's work, worries that it was all pointless, dies, and then discovers that the beauty he was trying to create actually mattered.


## Explanation
The story can be seen as an allegory for the creative process and the afterlife. Niggle's journey reflects the idea that the value of art and effort is not always immediately apparent, and that true fulfillment may come after one's earthly life.

Niggle is a painter obsessed with painting a huge Tree. He spends enormous amounts of time perfecting tiny details—especially individual leaves.

Most readers see Niggle as a version of J. R. R. Tolkien himself. Tolkien spent decades building Middle-earth, endlessly revising languages, histories, maps, and stories. He often feared he would never finish his work.
Even the name "Niggle" is meaningful. In English, to "niggle" means to fuss over small details.

## How it's resonate with creative people
Many readers focus less on the religious symbolism and more on the feeling of being Niggle:

- You have a huge vision in your head.
- Real life constantly interrupts.
- The work never feels finished.
- You worry nobody understands what you're trying to make.
- You fear you'll run out of time.

Tolkien himself felt this intensely while working on Middle-earth.

## Conclusion
**Niggle spends his life believing that his masterpiece is unfinished and inadequate. The revelation is that the value was never in producing a perfect painting. The value was in seeing the Tree at all.

That's why the final mood isn't sadness about an unfinished work—it's gratitude that the vision was real.**
